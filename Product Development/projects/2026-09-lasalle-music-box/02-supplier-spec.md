# Stage 2 — Supplier Product Specification: Xiamen Ling Er Gong Chuang Music Box

**Source:** owner-supplied facts (not yet a `suppliers/{id}` record in the
app — see open question below) + the four reference product photos of the
supplier's existing city-souvenir line (Wuhan, Nanjing, Chongqing, Xiamen).

**Base product:** dynamic/mechanical music box (动态八音盒)
**Supplier:** Xiamen Ling Er Gong Chuang (厦门灵珥工创, exact registered name
unconfirmed — see open question)

## Material Truth

- **Body:** MDF (laser-cut, tab-and-slot construction — visible in the
  reference photos' finger-jointed corners, no glue seams).
- **Print:** UV printing directly onto the MDF lid panel (confirmed by the
  supplier's own marketing image: "优质UV印刷工艺... 色彩鲜艳、饱和度高、层次丰富、
  立体感强" — vivid, high-saturation, layered color, strong dimensionality).
- **Mechanism:** metal wind-up music movement + a metal turn-knob on one
  side face (the supplier's own image labels this "耐用金属旋钮" — durable
  metal knob).
- **A raised centerpiece element** appears on every reference box — a
  separately die-cut, layered plaque (the city name, sitting proud of the
  lid surface, sometimes on a rotating disc like a turntable on the
  Xiamen/Chongqing boxes) — this is a real recurring construction feature,
  not just an illustration choice. Worth confirming if this is a standard,
  no-extra-cost feature of this product line or an upcharge.

## Size Logic

- **Dimensions:** 88mm × 71mm × 42mm (per the supplier's own spec sheet
  image: 8.7cm × 7.1cm × 4.2cm) — a low, wide rectangular box, lid facing
  up.
- **Aspect ratio implication:** the lid is a roughly 5:4 landscape rectangle
  — wide enough for a horizon-line scene (as every reference example uses:
  sky/background at top, ground-level landmarks across the middle,
  foreground detail at the bottom edge), not a tall/vertical composition.
- The **raised centerpiece plaque** sits right-of-center on every reference
  example, roughly at the lid's mid-height — a strong precedent for where
  La Salle's own centerpiece element (the star? the shield? "La Salle"
  wordmark?) should probably sit.

## Printability / Production Constraints

- The reference line's actual illustration style, read directly from the
  four examples: **flat-vector cartoon cityscape**, semi-isometric/gently
  angled perspective (not flat-on), saturated candy-bright color palette,
  clean black linework outlines, soft cel-shading (not photoreal, not
  painterly) — landmark buildings + local motifs (cherry blossoms for
  Wuhan, mountains/skyline for Chongqing, a temple/bridge for Nanjing)
  arranged as a **collage**, not a single unified scene with correct
  real-world geography.
- Fine detail holds up well in these UV-printed examples (window grids on
  buildings, individual cherry blossoms, small text) — this process is NOT
  the limiting factor an engraving/etching process would be. The real
  constraint is keeping the composition inside the lid's flat printable
  area and not relying on texture/gradient effects that would fight the
  flat-vector house style of this product line.
- The **die-cut centerpiece plaque** is a separate physical piece — its
  edge is a hard silhouette cut, so whatever shape La Salle's centerpiece
  becomes (a star? a shield?) needs a silhouette clean enough to actually
  die-cut, not a busy illustrated shape.

   MOQ) or per-unit, and whether the school has a specific tune in mind
