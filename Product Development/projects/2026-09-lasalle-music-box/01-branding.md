# Stage 1 — Customer Branding Elements: La Salle Primary School

**Source used:** owner-supplied photos (school crest, building exterior ×2,
stained glass, mosaic, swimming pool, students in PE kit, mural) +
`school_logo.svg` (wordmark asset, colors only extracted — see note below).
**Confidence: high** — real photographed assets, not memory.

## What the crest actually shows (read directly from the image)

A shield, quartered:
- **Top-left (red quarter):** a gold star radiating light rays at the top,
  an open book and a lit oil lamp/candle below it, with the Chinese
  characters **克己復禮** ("restrain the self, return to propriety" — a
  Confucian classical phrase, i.e. self-discipline + right conduct).
- **Top-right (blue quarter):** a gold zigzag/chevron mark (reads as a
  stylized mountain or "M"-like device).
- Flanking the shield: a **green laurel branch** (left) and **holly leaves**
  (right) — classic heraldic side ornaments.
- Below the shield, on a scroll banner: **"FIDES ET OPERA"** — Latin for
  "Faith and Zeal/Work," the standard Lasallian Brothers' schools' motto.

## Core Motifs

- **The star** (Signum Fidei-style radiating star) — geometry: sharp
  radial symmetry, light rays fanning from a point. This is the single
  most distinctive, most "brandable" shape on the crest.
- **Open book + lit lamp** — geometry: a low horizontal book shape with a
  small vertical flame above it. Reads instantly as "school/learning."
- **The shield itself** — a strong, simple heraldic silhouette; quartered
  red/blue.
- **Laurel + holly flanking** — symmetrical framing device, could translate
  into a border/frame motif rather than a central subject.
- **The curved modern school building** — a distinctive rounded white
  facade, recognizable as "this specific school" to anyone who's seen it,
  separate from the (generic-to-Lasallian-schools) crest.
- **De La Salle founder + schoolboys** (stained glass) — a specific
  Catholic/Lasallian devotional image; use with care (see open question).

## Typography Tone

The crest's Latin motto is formal, collegiate, heraldic. The student
photos (PE kit, jumping, "We Are The CHAMPION" pool banner) are the
opposite: energetic, proud, playful. **This is a real tension to resolve in
Stage 3**, not an oversight — a graduation souvenir for 12-year-olds
probably wants the energetic register more than the heraldic one, but the
crest is what makes it unmistakably "La Salle" rather than generic.

## Open questions for Heymans / the school (don't guess these)

1. **Crest vs. wordmark vs. mascot-illustration:** does the school have a
   preference, or existing merchandise precedent, for which brand asset
   leads (the formal crest, the `school_logo.svg` wordmark, or a friendlier
   illustrated mark)? P6 merchandise for 12-year-olds may call for
   something less heraldic than the crest alone.
2. **Religious imagery:** the stained glass and mosaic (De La Salle, Christ)
   are real assets but are specifically Catholic devotional images — using
   them on a commercial souvenir for a school PTA may be entirely
   appropriate (many Lasallian schools do), but this is a judgment call for
   the school/PTA to make, not one to assume.
3. **English name/wordmark:** "La Salle Primary School" (English) /
   喇沙小學 (Chinese) — confirm which name treatment, and whether a
   graduation year ("Class of 2027"?) or "P6" is expected on the piece,
   the way the supplier's city-souvenir line always names the city.
