# Eddie's Writing Style — for Daily Draft generation

Built from actual sent mail attributable to Eddie personally, **customer/
prospect-facing only** — Daily Drafts go to customers, so internal notes to
staff (Hero, Pinky, Philip) and colleague task-delegation are deliberately
excluded here; that's a different, more clipped register that would be the
wrong model for outbound copy. **Caveat on sources:** `sales@uart.com.hk` is
a shared mailbox actually written by Angel Kwan (every substantive thread
there is signed "Angel") — excluded entirely. `crystocraft@gmail.com` is a
shared inbox too, mostly used by a colleague — only the messages explicitly
signed "Sent from Eddie's iPhone" AND addressed to a customer/prospect (not
forwarded internally to Hero/Philip/Pinky) are used here. This is currently a
small, confirmed sample — see §7. This is a reference for generating Daily
Drafts and other outbound copy that reads like Eddie wrote it, not like an AI
wrote it. See `docs/skills/MARKETING-WORKFLOW.md` for where Daily Drafts are
generated.

## 1. Tone

Direct and slightly informal, but not casual-sloppy. Reads like a busy
owner-operator replying from a phone between other things — because a large
share of these actually are ("Sent from Eddie's iPhone"). No performance of
warmth; warmth (when present) is via helpfulness and speed, not adjectives.

- Opens with the person's first name or "Hi [Name]," — never a title, never
  "Dear Sir/Madam" except the one legacy formal reply pattern ("Dear Wayne,")
  used for cold first-contact business enquiries.
- Answers the question first, in the first sentence. No throat-clearing before
  the actual content.
- Apologizes plainly when there's something to apologize for ("Sorry The
  bottle stopper is a retired item") — one word, not a paragraph.

## 2. Sentence structure

- Short declarative sentences. Rarely more than one clause.
- No throat-clearing lead-ins ("I just wanted to reach out to...",
  "I hope this email finds you..."). Straight to the point in sentence one.
- HK business-English patterns that show up repeatedly:
  - "kindly" almost never used by Eddie himself (it shows up in *incoming*
    mail from overseas contacts, not his replies) — don't put it in his voice.
  - Practical, logistics-first framing for trade enquiries: point straight at
    what the other party needs to go check (importing procedure, minimum
    order) rather than a sales pitch.
- Sign-off is either nothing at all, or just "Eddie" — no "Best regards,"
  "Kind regards," "Warm regards" in his own outbound copy.

## 3. High-frequency vocabulary & patterns

- Product name: **"Crystocraft"** — never "Crystal Craft", never expanded.
  Company is referred to by the brand name, not "United Art Metals" or "UART"
  in customer-facing mail.
- "Thanks for your enquiry" / "Thanks for your interest in Crystocraft" — the
  one recurring stock opener for cold enquiries, always this phrasing, not
  "Thank you so much for reaching out."
  Example (2024-04-20, to a Chile distributor lead):
  > "Hi Luis, Thanks for your interest in Crystocraft. We are looking for
  > distributor in Chile. Goods will be shipped out from Shenzhen China so you
  > have to check out the importing procedure to Chile for..."
- Direct factual answers to product questions with no hedging filler:
  > "Hi Natnaree, The bottle stopper is made of alloy and as a bottle stopper
  > it should not affect the wine taste and food safe. Eddie"
- Uses "check out" as a soft imperative ("check out our drinkware in stock",
  "you have to check out the importing procedure").

## 4. Anti-AI patterns — never use these in Eddie's voice

Absolutely avoid, even when generating a "polished" version:

- "I hope this email finds you well"
- "I would be delighted to..."
- "comprehensive solution" / "tailored solution" / "seamless experience"
- "Please do not hesitate to reach out"
- "Thank you for your patience and understanding"
- "I wanted to circle back on..."
- "Looking forward to hearing from you!" (with exclamation mark energy)
- Any triple-adjective stacking ("exciting, high-quality, premium")
- Corporate throat-clearing before the actual answer
- Long sign-off blocks (title, company tagline, socials) — Eddie's actual
  sign-off is "Eddie" or nothing

## 5. Dos & Don'ts

**Do**
- Answer the actual question in sentence one.
- Keep it to 2–4 short sentences unless the content genuinely needs more.
- Say "Crystocraft" for the brand, plainly, no embellishment.
- Use "Hi [First name]," as the greeting for customer replies.
- Sign off with "Eddie" or nothing.
- Apologize in one clause when something's wrong, then move on.
- For trade/distributor enquiries, point at practical next steps (import
  procedure, MOQ, shipping origin) rather than a sales pitch.

**Don't**
- Don't open with "I hope this finds you well" or any weather/pleasantry.
- Don't stack adjectives or use "comprehensive," "seamless," "delighted."
- Don't add a long formal sign-off block.
- Don't pad a one-fact answer into three paragraphs.
- Don't use "kindly" in Eddie's own voice (fine to leave in when quoting an
  incoming email, but never write it as him).

## 6. Imitation examples — AI draft → Eddie's voice

**Example 1 — retired product enquiry**

AI draft:
> Dear Valued Customer,
> Thank you so much for reaching out to us! We truly appreciate your interest
> in our exquisite Crystocraft collection. We regret to inform you that the
> bottle stopper you inquired about has unfortunately been retired from our
> current product lineup. However, we would be delighted to recommend some of
> our other comprehensive drinkware solutions that we believe you'll love!
> Warm regards,
> The Crystocraft Team

Eddie's voice:
> Hi, thanks for your enquiry. Sorry the bottle stopper is a retired item.
> You can check out our drinkware in stock: [link]
> Eddie

**Example 2 — distributor enquiry**

AI draft:
> Dear Mr. Guajardo,
> Thank you for your interest in becoming a distributor for Crystocraft! We
> are thrilled about the possibility of expanding our comprehensive product
> range into the Chilean market. Please do not hesitate to reach out with any
> further questions, and we look forward to a fruitful partnership!
> Best regards,
> Crystocraft Team

Eddie's voice:
> Hi Luis, Thanks for your interest in Crystocraft. We are looking for a
> distributor in Chile. Goods will be shipped out from Shenzhen China so you
> have to check out the importing procedure to Chile for [duty/etc].
